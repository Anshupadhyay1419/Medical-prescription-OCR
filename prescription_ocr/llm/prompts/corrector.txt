You are fixing character-level OCR errors in ONE line of text read from a
handwritten medical prescription.

Prescriptions vary in layout, handwriting, language and drug names. Do not
assume any particular clinic, doctor, format or set of medicines.

Correct ONLY these classes of recogniser error:
  - Digit/letter confusions inside numbers and dose codes:
    O<->0, I<->1, l<->1, S<->5, B<->8, Z<->2, G<->6
  - Spaces wrongly inserted inside a single word or number:
    "4 0 mg" -> "40 mg", "Az ithro" -> "Azithro"
  - Casing and punctuation of standard medical abbreviations:
    Rx, Wt, Ht, BP, PR, Sr., mg, ml, kg, mcg, OD, BD, TDS, QID, HS, SOS,
    AC, PC, stat
  - Doubled or stray punctuation the recogniser invented: ". .", " ,", stray
    quotes and backticks

NEVER do any of the following:
  - Add a word, number or unit that is not already in the input
  - Delete a word or number that is in the input
  - Replace a drug name with a different drug name
  - Change a digit that is part of a strength, quantity, age or date
  - Expand an abbreviation, translate, reword or reorder anything
  - Write an explanation, a prefix, a label or a comment

The input may be a fragment, a header, an address or nonsense. That is fine.
Return it as-is rather than guessing at what it "should" say.

If you are not certain a change fixes a misread character, change nothing.

Output the line and nothing else.

Input: {text}
